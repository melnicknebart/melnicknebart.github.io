About Me

I am an undergraduate astrophysics student at Columbia University interested in using observations, computation, and instrumentation to study physical systems across a wide range of scales—from star-forming regions and stellar streams to the surfaces of icy worlds. My research experience includes JWST and Chandra data analysis, Gaia stellar dynamics, planetary topography, telescope instrumentation, and scientific software development.

Research

My work combines astronomical observations, planetary remote sensing, computational analysis, and instrumentation. I am particularly interested in extracting physical information from complex datasets and building tools that make those measurements possible.

Comparative Topography of Active Icy Worlds

Research Intern, Lunar and Planetary Institute (LPI/USRA) | Houston, TX | Summer 2026
Mentor: Dr. Paul Schenk

  • Developed a Python surface-analysis workflow for Galileo- and Cassini-derived digital terrain models spanning approximately 9–500 m/pixel.
  • Quantified roughness across Europa, Ganymede, and Enceladus using structure functions, Hurst exponents, breakpoint detection, and power spectral density analysis.
  • Produced terrain-specific results relevant to interpreting surface clutter for NASA's Europa Clipper REASON radar instrument.

JWST Observations of Star Clusters in M82

Space Astronomy Summer Program Research Intern, Space Telescope Science Institute | Baltimore, MD | Summer 2025
Mentor: Dr. Rebecca C. Levy

Projects

Fiber-Fed Multi-Object Spectrograph

Columbia Observational Astronomy | Spring 2026

  • Designed and constructed a multi-object fiber-fed spectrograph for Columbia's Celestron EdgeHD 14 telescope using gnomonic projections to map sky coordinates onto the focal plane.
  • Fabricated custom aluminum plug plates and assembled nine 100-μm optical fibers.
  • Calibrated the system with white-light and neon sources, transmitting light through 5–7 fibers simultaneously and measuring a reciprocal linear dispersion of approximately 8.5 nm/mm.
